# Symptom-to-Department Mapping Guide

This guide helps map common presenting symptoms to the most appropriate medical
department for triage.

## Cardiovascular
Chest pain, chest tightness, pressure or squeezing in the chest, palpitations,
irregular heartbeat, shortness of breath on exertion, fainting (syncope), and
swelling in the legs or ankles map to **Cardiology**. Chest pain radiating to
the left arm or jaw with sweating and nausea should be treated as a potential
heart attack and routed to **Emergency** immediately.

## Neurological
Persistent or severe headaches, migraines with aura, seizures, sudden numbness
or weakness (especially one-sided), difficulty speaking, vision changes,
dizziness, and loss of balance map to **Neurology**. Sudden severe headache
("worst headache of my life"), facial drooping, or slurred speech are stroke red
flags and must go to **Emergency**.

## Dermatological
Skin rashes, acne, eczema, psoriasis, persistent itching, changing moles, hair
loss, and nail disorders map to **Dermatology**. A rapidly spreading rash with
fever, blistering, or difficulty breathing may indicate a severe allergic
reaction and should be routed to **Emergency**.

## Respiratory
Chronic cough, wheezing, shortness of breath at rest, and recurrent chest
infections map to **General Practice** for initial assessment, then **Pulmonology**.

## When to escalate to Emergency
Any of the following is an emergency regardless of department: difficulty
breathing at rest, chest pain with sweating/nausea, sudden weakness or speech
difficulty, uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness, or a severe allergic
reaction.
